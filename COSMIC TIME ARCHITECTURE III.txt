19. Promotion Ladder — How a Pattern Earns More Meaning

Not every interesting relation deserves the same status.

Timekeeper therefore uses a staged promotion rule.

A claim may move upward only when the obligations of the next layer are independently satisfied.

The ladder is:

[
\boxed{
\text{observation}
\rightarrow
\text{recurrence}
\rightarrow
\text{episode}
\rightarrow
\text{enrichment}
\rightarrow
\text{mechanism}
\rightarrow
\text{interpretation}
}
]

No rung may borrow evidence from a higher rung in order to justify itself.

---

Level 0 — Observation

A relation has been noticed.

Examples:

- two angles are close;
- a number repeats;
- several bodies form a visually interesting configuration;
- a date lies near a recurrence peak;
- two mathematical constructions return similar values.

The permitted claim is:

[
\boxed{\text{this relation was observed}.}
]

Nothing more is implied.

Similarity is not identity.

Adjacency is not coupling.

Recognition is not mechanism.

The relation survives a declared witness, tolerance, source, and scoring rule.

For a module C:

[
D_C(t)<\epsilon.
]

The recurrence must survive re-execution from the frozen source data.

The permitted claim becomes:

[
\boxed{
\text{this declared structure recurs under witness }\mathcal V.
}
]

This still does not establish unusual frequency, common control, or physical causation.

The observed recurrence or episode structure exceeds an appropriate frozen null.

The relevant question is no longer:

- translated windows;
- matched-rate controls;
- shuffled labels;

Promotion requires the chosen statistic and null to be specified before interpretation.

If the null survives, the result stays below this rung.

Thus the current planetary result:

[

[
\boxed{\text{global excess synchronization: yes}}.

The ladder does not move merely because the pattern is beautiful.

20. Witness Fidelity — How Far a Result May Speak

A witness is not globally strong or weak.

Its fidelity is relative to the claim being made.

For a declared claim C and witness \mathcal V, use the governing rule:

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Precision}(C)
\le
\operatorname{Fidelity}(\mathcal V,C).
}
]

Claim precision must not exceed the fidelity of the witness to the source distinction relevant to that claim.

Witness evaluation keeps three properties separate:

- fidelity — does the witness preserve the distinction relevant to this claim?
- resolution — how finely can the witness distinguish among the relevant alternatives?
- provenance strength — how directly is the witness bound to the source, event, transformation, or trajectory it purports to record?

These properties do not form an authority ladder.

[
\boxed{
\text{witness fidelity is claim-relative}
\qquad
\text{and}
\qquad
\text{fidelity}\neq\text{authority}.
}
]

A witness may be strong for one proposition and weak for another.

Thus \mathcal V_{\mathrm{DIR}} may support the claim that a directed module recurred without supporting the claim that a physical coupling caused the recurrence.

\mathcal V_{\mathrm{SHELL}} may support shell recurrence while being unable to recover handedness already erased by its projection.

\mathcal V_{\mathrm{HIST}} may establish that a frozen geometric episode overlaps a historical interval while remaining unable, by that fact alone, to establish historical causation.
