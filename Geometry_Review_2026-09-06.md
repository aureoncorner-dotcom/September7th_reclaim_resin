### Specific image/source repairs

1. **GQG subgroup arithmetic.** The v0.13 image states |G|=24 and |H|=2 while showing G/H with two classes, each a 12-state orbit. For an ordinary subgroup/coset construction, |G/H|=24/2=12. To obtain two 12-element cosets, H must have order 12. The retrieved OMNIBUS review gives the consistent example: G=C6×C4, diagonal H of order 12, quotient C2. Two orbits of a general action alone would not justify calling them cosets or writing a quotient kernel.

2. **The 72 register.** C12×C2×C3 has 72 elements and exponent 12. It is not the cyclic group C72. The old card's warning on this point is correct; preserve it.

3. **Lantern branch mismatch.** The poster reads 12 → 47 → 8642 → 18 → 13579 → 12 → π. The retrieved Trident reference instead reads 12 → 47 → 4723 → 18 → 13579 → 12 → π. These are different traces. Neither is silently substituted for the other here.

5. **The “13 chambers” picture.** The attached Trident ring visibly skips label 7 and contains twelve pictured stations. The source's actual thirteen chamber names are also different from the pictured generic workflow functions. The poster is not a faithful enumeration of that source ring.

6. **Closure status.** A located counterexample is FAILED for the named claim; an untested case is UNRESOLVED. The older planetary poster's combined “NO / UNTESTED → UNRESOLVED” branch should be separated in any successor.

7. **Recorded percentages.** Lantern's 99.78% stability and 86.42% field strength, the √3 poster's stress figures, and GQG's health numbers have no supplied calibration or data trace. Treat them as illustration text unless the underlying measurement record is recovered.

## 3. Executed here: the simplest Jane/Lantern compilation

**Declared new model, not a recovered physical meaning:** read each whole number token as one slot, advance one slot per tick, and repeat the displayed trace cyclically. “13579” remains one token here, not five separate events. For Jane, the resulting word is:

| 12 | 12, 13579, 8642 |

Therefore the current token is not a sufficient autonomous state.

**Jane: 5 → 10 → 12 → 12.**

All twelve positions have different infinite future token sequences. Any exact deterministic state representation that retains these future outputs must distinguish the twelve positions. Retaining the slot index supplies the repair. The compiler uses the declared word to derive these distinctions; an actual predictor does not need to peek at future observations.

These are working finite sequence models. They neither prove physical locking nor require an infinite-state engine. In contrast, the golden 39-screen's irrational slip frequency rules out a deterministic finite autonomous generator for its entire infinite sequence. Similar-looking diagrams can encode very different dynamics.

### What the speed variants could test

If “2×” or “√3×” multiplies every component's continuous-time rate, it merely changes how fast the same path is traversed. A periodic orbit remains periodic, with its period divided by that multiplier. If only one component is accelerated relative to an unchanged clock, the relative motion changes. An irrational relative ratio can remove an exact common period.

These are different models. The picture supplies no rule selecting one. It also does not justify scaling amplitude when scaling time. A useful implementation should expose which clock is changed and keep that choice in the record.

## 4. Executed here: parity failure in the newest cosine kernel

This is a fresh calculation from **TD-COS-FH-001**, not a claim copied from the earlier Villain result.

Use today's default J=1, t=1/2, h6=0 and 2×2×2 periodic torus. There are V=8 vertices and B=8V+7=71 microticks per sweep. Let Γx be the specified two-edge reference cycle.

Compare these admissible positive-weight states:

- X0: I=0, M=0, q=000.
- X1: I=2Γx, M=0, q=000.

Both satisfy integer current conservation and the mod-two membrane constraint. They have the same sector q but different signed winding: Wx=0 versus Wx=2.

Write I_n(1) for the modified Bessel function. Conditional on a unit x-cycle proposal, the toggle probabilities are

    p0 = [I_1(1)/I_0(1)]²
    p1 = {1 + [I_3(1)/I_2(1)]²}/2.

For X0, both signs create unit-magnitude currents on the two edges. For X1, the negative sign reduces both currents from 2 to 1 and is accepted; the positive sign increases them from 2 to 3 with the displayed ratio.

Using exact rational lower and upper bounds for the positive Bessel series, I obtained:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| p0 | 0.19926400165310924025… |
| p1 | 0.51333444402451280363… |
| Difference in full microtick projected laws | 0.00442352735734371216… |
| Unavoidable worst-case total-variation error of one shared reduced law | at least 0.00221176367867185608… |

The unit-sector family has probability 3/71, and chooses x with probability 1/3, so its x-axis weight is 1/71. Every other move family retains q; y/z sector probabilities agree for these two states. Thus the exact full-microtick projected-law distance is (p1−p0)/71. Half this distance lower-bounds the error of any one law assigned to both states by the triangle inequality.

**Conclusion:** At its default reference point, today's complete cosine microtick kernel does not strongly lump through q alone. Numerically the two next-sector laws differ by about **0.442 percentage points** in total variation; every one-law replacement must miss at least one by **0.221 percentage points** or more.

The strict inequality has an elementary analytical check too. At J=1, the positive series gives I1/I0<1/2, so p0<1/4. The downhill proposal in X1 is accepted and the uphill one has positive probability, so p1>1/2. Hence p1>p0 without depending on rounded numbers.

This is a proof about two explicit states and the specified one-attempt kernel. It is not a sampler run, mixing result, all-parameter theorem, or a proof about the 71-step sweep projection. It does not claim that adding signed winding alone closes the whole sampler. It does show exactly why sector parity is insufficient and why this coarse-state failure is compatible with valid full-state sampling.

### Reproduction of the numerical enclosure

For n≥0 and J=1, use a_k=1/[2^(2k+n) k!(n+k)!]. Sum through K=30. With r=1/[4(K+1)(n+K+1)], the exact rational enclosure is

    S_K ≤ I_n(1) ≤ S_K + a_K r/(1−r).

Apply interval division and squaring to the positive ratios above. The resulting total-variation enclosure width was below 1.5×10^−89; the lower endpoint was positive. The calculation ran using Python's standard-library Fraction and Decimal.

The Bessel definitions and generating identity agree with [NIST DLMF 10.25.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.25.E2) and [10.35.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.35.E1). These support the special-function step; the projected-kernel comparison is the new calculation here.

Bring back the **Jane/Lantern trace vocabulary and Trident's replaceable workflow functions** as an interface to explicit models. The strongest immediate prototype has:

- Separate original Jane, poster Lantern, and Trident-source variants.
- A current cycle-position state and displayed token.

- A record of same-label states with different successors.
- A minimal-state compiler for finite models.

The retrieved chamber traces supply none of those. The task here has produced a usable mathematical interpretation without pretending that those missing physical definitions were already present.
