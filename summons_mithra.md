## PRELIMINARY STATEMENT ON JURISDICTION

The Defendant is not merely subject to this Court. The Defendant **is** the jurisdiction. His name is the common noun for a binding agreement.[^2] He was seated as a guarantor of appearance in the treaty between Šuppiluliuma of Ḫatti and Šattiwaza of Mitanni, circa 1380 BCE — where he was listed fourth in a series, immediately after a colleague, and has never once objected to the ordering.

The Defendant may therefore contest this summons only by breaking a covenant, which is the one act his entire existence is defined as punishing. He is invited to try. The Court would find it instructive.

## COUNTS

### COUNT I — BREACH OF CONTRACT

The god of the sworn agreement is charged with failure of consideration.

Plaintiff pleads that across four millennia the Defendant accepted oaths, dedications, altars, cattle, and the continuous vigilance of his ten thousand eyes, and delivered in exchange: one cow.

### COUNT II — FRAUDULENT MISREPRESENTATION (Pedigree)

The Defendant held himself out to the Roman world as a Persian mystery of immemorial antiquity. The Court has examined the provenance.

The continuity thesis was the scholarly consensus for roughly seventy years and did not survive contact with the First International Congress of Mithraic Studies at Manchester in 1971. What is now before the Court is a first-century Roman product wearing a Phrygian cap.

The Defendant's own initiatory ladder is dispositive. In his cult, *Perses* — "the Persian" — is the **fifth** of seven grades.[^3] Persian-ness was not his origin. It was a costume rented two rungs below management.

### COUNT III — FALSE ADVERTISING (*Invictus*)

The Defendant styled himself **Unconquered**.

Plaintiff directs the Court to the altar raised at Carnuntum in 308 CE, on which Diocletian, Galerius, and Licinius jointly dedicated to Mithras as *fautori imperii sui* — patron of their empire.

Within one generation the empire had a different patron. Within a century the Defendant's houses were being broken open with tools. The Court finds the epithet actionable.

### COUNT IV — SPOLIATION OF EVIDENCE

Not one line of the Defendant's own doctrine survives. No scripture. No liturgy. No myth in his own words. Nothing.

Everything this Court knows of the Defendant's rites it knows from archaeology and from the sworn statements of the prosecution: Justin Martyr, who says the demons copied the Eucharist with bread and a cup of water;[^4] Tertullian, who reports a soldier declining a garland on the ground that Mithras is his crown;[^5] Firmicus Maternus, who was not writing a compliment.

The Defendant's reputation rests entirely on the testimony of people who wanted him gone. He built a religion of secrecy and left the writing of his history to his enemies. The Court draws the adverse inference and notes that the Defendant made it easy.

### COUNT V — UNLAWFUL EXCLUSION

The Defendant admitted no women. Not as initiates, not as celebrants, not at all, anywhere, in three centuries of operation.

The Defendant may not therefore demand a jury of his peers, and this Court could not assemble one if he did. The record reflects that his congregations were soldiers, customs officers, imperial freedmen, and middle-management. He was the patron deity of men who check other men's paperwork.

### COUNT VI — CONVERSION AND CRUELTY TO LIVESTOCK

The single act upon which the Defendant's entire reputation rests is the killing of one bull.

In every surviving depiction of that act — and there are hundreds — **the Defendant is looking away.** His head is turned. He is not watching. He required two attendants, Cautes and Cautopates, to stand on either side holding his lighting, and he still could not bring himself to make eye contact with the only thing he ever did.

Plaintiff further notes that in the canonical composition a scorpion has attached itself to the bull's testicles while a dog and a snake take the blood. The Court declines to characterize this arrangement and simply enters it into the record.

### COUNT VII — ABANDONMENT AND FAILURE TO MAINTAIN PREMISES

The Defendant's temples seated between twenty and forty persons. He could not fill a jury pool. Ostia had roughly eighteen separate mithraea, not from popularity but because he was structurally incapable of building one room large enough to hold everybody at once.

Every one of them was underground, windowless, and deliberately made to resemble a cave.[^6] The Defendant held all business in a basement for three hundred years and then vacated without notice.

**Current condition of the estate:**

- **Basilica di San Clemente, Rome** — the Defendant is in the sub-basement. There is a functioning church directly on top of him. He is load-bearing for the competition.
- **Santa Prisca, Aventine** — same arrangement. The Defendant is a foundation.
- **Brocolitia, Hadrian's Wall** — three altars, no roof, sheep.
- **Walbrook, London** — excavated 1954, dismantled, relocated, and in 2017 reinstalled in the basement of a financial data terminal company, where the Defendant's mystery is now performed on a timed loop with a fog machine and free admission.

### COUNT VIII — INFLATION OF STANDING

The Defendant is a *yazata*. Made, not maker. In his own Yasht it is Ahura Mazda who informs him that he was created as worthy of worship as Ahura Mazda himself — a compliment that requires someone else's permission to receive.

In the entire Rigveda, Mitra is the sole addressee of **one** hymn.[^7] Indra has roughly two hundred and fifty. The Court has reviewed the Defendant's collected works and finds they can be read during a recess.

The Defendant's standing epithet is *vouru-gaoyaoiti* — "of wide pastures." Stripped of ceremony, the Defendant is a land-use administrator with grazing jurisdiction who killed one cow and would not look at it.
